Making food from air

Read everything on this page carefully. You will need all of it for the worksheet.

Where does a tree's mass come from?

A huge river red gum can weigh many tonnes. Most people guess the mass comes from the soil. They are wrong.

It comes mostly from the air. The carbon and much of the oxygen in wood come from carbon dioxide. The hydrogen comes from water. Soil adds only small amounts of minerals.

The evidence

In the 1600s, Jan van Helmont grew a willow tree in a pot of soil for 5 years, giving it only water.

The tree gained about 74 kg. The soil lost only about 57 g.

He thought the mass came from the water. We now know most of it came from carbon dioxide in the air.

Photosynthesis: inputs and outputs

Inputs (reactants)

  • Carbon dioxide from the air, entering through tiny holes in leaves called stomata
  • Water from the soil, through the roots
  • Light energy from the Sun

Where

In chloroplasts, mostly in leaf cells. The green pigment chlorophyll captures the light energy.

Outputs (products)

  • Glucose: a sugar that stores energy and carbon
  • Oxygen: released into the air through the stomata
carbon dioxide + water light energy→chlorophyll glucose + oxygen
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light is energy, not matter. It drives the reaction but it is not made of atoms, so it is written above the arrow, not as a reactant.

Photosynthesis in the carbon cycle

Photosynthesis moves carbon from the atmosphere into the biosphere. It is an interaction between spheres, and it is the main way carbon enters living things.

Photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the air. Respiration, decomposition and combustion add CO₂ back.

Carbon stored in biomass

Glucose is built into wood, leaves and roots. Biomass is the mass of living material. About half the dry mass of wood is carbon.

A big old tree can hold carbon for hundreds of years. Seagrass and mangroves, like those in Gulf St Vincent, store carbon in the mud beneath them. This is called blue carbon.

Day and night

Plants respire all the time, day and night. Respiration uses glucose and oxygen and releases CO₂. Photosynthesis only happens when there is light.

  • Bright light: photosynthesis is faster than respiration, so the plant takes in more CO₂ than it releases.
  • Dim light: the two can happen at the same rate, so CO₂ stays about the same.
  • Dark: no photosynthesis, only respiration, so the plant releases CO₂.

    Explain how photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and stores carbon in the biosphere.

    Include the word equation and one example.

    Model answer. Claim: Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and stores the carbon in living things. Process: Plants take in carbon dioxide through their stomata and water through their roots. In the chloroplasts, chlorophyll captures light energy, which is used to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen (carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen). The oxygen is released into the air. Example: A river red gum builds the glucose into wood, leaves and roots, so its carbon can be stored as biomass for hundreds of years. Link: This is an interaction between spheres, because carbon moves from the atmosphere into the biosphere. It is the main way carbon enters the biosphere in the carbon cycle.
